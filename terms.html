3. Backups and restores

The backup feature is optional and off until you enable it. Three things about it are worth stating plainly rather than burying:

  • It is a backup, not a sync. You can restore a backup onto a new device and continue there. Using two devices at the same time is not supported: the most recent backup will overwrite the other on restore.
  • There is no passphrase to set or lose. Backups are encrypted on your device before upload, with a key the app derives itself. You are never asked to invent or remember one, and there is nothing you can forget that would lock you out of your own backup — a reinstall, or a brand-new phone, can restore from your Drive without any secret you have to keep.
  • What that encryption is and is not for. It protects the backup file in transit and at rest in your Drive, so a copy obtained on its own is not readable as plain text. Because the key is derived by the app rather than from a secret only you know, it is not designed to withstand someone who has both the backup file and the ability to inspect the app. If you want protection stronger than that, do not rely on the backup feature for it.

We make no guarantee that a backup will complete at any particular time. Scheduled backups depend on your device's operating system, network and battery conditions, and Android may defer background work. A backup is a safety net, not a contractual commitment.
